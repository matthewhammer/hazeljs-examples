Pinball, its physics in Hazel

Hazel activities › Physics simulation · source on GitHub · hazeljs on npm

Ten boards, each a piece of Hazel data: its walls, bumpers, flippers, portals and lamps. Many have chambers with flippers of their own, whose gutters drop to the chamber below: every left flipper moves with ⬅️, every right one with ➡️. Every frame, a Hazel program moves the ball: gravity, then the walls, the bumpers and the flippers it meets, and the portals, which hold it a moment before it comes out. Launch it soft, mid or full (the 1, 2, 3 keys), or hold 🚀 (or the space bar) and let go, the longer the harder. The flippers are ⬅️ and ➡️, or the ← and → keys (or Z and /), or a tap on the table: on a paddle, or anywhere on its side. A ball lost within 8 seconds of its launch comes back, once; light every lamp in a row to make it blink. Three balls a game.
