# hazeljs-examples: versions

Each version lists what the apps do as of that version. The newest version is first.

## 0.0.72 (2026-11-22)

- **Gnome dig: bedrock, more of it in later levels** (Matthew: "okay the new sunlight dynamic makes the levels more fun --- let's also add, for more challenging levels (more so in later levels), some squares of 'bedrock' that cannot be dug --- preventing sunlight from reaching ghosts under them"). A new kind of cell in the rules (`Dig.hz`, cell 3): grey stone that nothing digs, not you, not the monsters, and that the sunlight does not pass. From the second level on, a run of it five long may lie over a monster's pocket, the row above (as likely as the level is high in tens: one in ten on the second level, always from the tenth), so the sun cannot be let in on it from above; and runs of 2 to 4 more lie here and there, one more every other level, up to 6. Runs never touch, even at a corner, so they never join into a wall; a gem walled off by bedrock and boulders is earth again; the diving gear's column has none.
- **The robot, adjusted for it.** It may now dig through an animal's cell when no boulder is anywhere above it, as letting it go there is safe; an animal walking out of the sunlight never walks under a loose or falling boulder; the robot goes to catch stunned monsters when no gem or gear is to be had; and for one stretch of 300 steps in every 1,800 it hunts the nearest awake monster first (with bedrock about, a monster could guard the last gems and keep it going to and fro). In the JavaScript copy of the rules, checked against Hazel (554 answers, none different), it cleared all 2,400 fresh levels, levels 1 to 12: no level stuck, no animal lost, no breath run out; on 44 a monster caught it once.

## 0.0.68 (2026-11-22)

The mole, the rabbit and the badger are now the rules' own (`Dig.hz`, a new `Animal` in the vocabulary): each level puts them in cells of earth by chance, the rabbit near the top, the mole in the middle, the badger low down. Dug out, an animal drops, a cell every four steps, until it lands; a boulder falling into an animal's cell loses the level: a life gone, and the level begins again, new. The monsters never burrow into an animal. The scene's burrows are dug round the animals where the rules put them. The robot keeps out of an animal's cell, and out of a cell under a boulder whose digging would let it fall onto one.
- **Gnome dig: ground water on some levels, and the diving gear** (Matthew: "let's include a ground-water element on some levels -- not all of them. the ground water forces the gnome to need diving equipment that's set in that level, at place where the gnome can get it before drowning, by expert play"). From the second level on, half the levels by chance have ground water from row 10 or 11 down: every dug cell there is under water. Without the diving gear you hold your breath, 100 steps of it (about a second and a half), shown as five bubbles over your head, the last two red; out of breath, you are caught as by a monster. Each such level's gear lies three or four cells under the water, in a column no boulder is in, so that digging straight down to it from the cell above the water takes 36 or 48 steps: expert play gets it on one breath. With it you stay under as long as you like, a mask on and an air tank on your back. The robot takes the gems above the water first, then dives straight down for the gear, coming up for air if it must, then takes the rest.
