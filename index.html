Ancient oracles, in Hazel

People have long asked chance a question and read what came back by fixed rules: how the lots are cast, what each one means, how they combine. Those rules are small programs. This series writes each oracle's rules in Hazel, a typed functional language with a live editor, and runs them in the page with hazeljs. Each oracle is its own app, on its own page: the reading on one side, revealed a step at a time, and on the other the Hazel program that makes it, in Hazel's own editor. Change the program and the next reading follows it.

I Ching

The Book of Changes: six lines cast by coins or yarrow

Tarot

78 cards, dealt into a spread and turned one by one

How each one works

The page keeps what is on the table and draws it; the Hazel program decides what happens. For each step the page passes the program some random numbers, and the program answers with what they mean in that oracle: a line of the hexagram, a card and where it lies. While the program has an error or a hole, the page's own JavaScript answers instead, and the page says which one did. The same pattern runs Messed Up Dress Up's scripts and a Petri net simulator.
